For most of human history, self-realization was a luxury.

You worried about food first. Then shelter. Then safety. If life was generous enough to provide all three, you could start worrying about belonging, recognition, and purpose.

The famous pyramid built from Abraham Maslow's hierarchy made intuitive sense because it described a world organized around scarcity. A small number of people reached the top because most people spent their lives fighting near the bottom.

I believe that pyramid is beginning to invert.

Human needs have obviously not disappeared. Billions of people still face hunger, violence, and material insecurity every day. Abundance is arriving unevenly, as every technological revolution does.

But the direction is clear: intelligence is becoming abundant, production is becoming cheaper, and access to the tools of creation is expanding at a speed humanity has never experienced.

That changes where more people can begin.

## Creation is moving to the bottom

A teenager with a cheap computer can learn from the best teachers in the world, write software, compose music, design a product, publish an idea, and reach millions of people.

Twenty years ago, each of those acts required a different institution. You needed a university, a company, a studio, a publisher, or a television network to give you permission.

Now you can start with a desire to express something.

AI makes this shift much more radical. Skill used to be the gate between intention and creation. You could imagine a game without knowing how to program it, hear a song without knowing how to play it, or understand a problem without having the technical ability to build its solution.

That gate is collapsing.

When creation becomes available before mastery, self-realization stops being the reward at the end of a successful life. It becomes one of the ways a life is built.

People will increasingly discover who they are by making things. Along the way, those creations can give them community, recognition, and even economic security. The journey starts at what used to be the top and works downward.

The old apex becomes a practical place to begin.

## Abundance removes a convenient excuse

This possibility is beautiful, but it is also uncomfortable.

Scarcity allowed us to postpone the question of what we wanted to do with our lives. First I need the degree. First I need the job. First I need enough money. Then, one distant day, I will make what I really want to make.

I know this excuse because I still catch myself using more sophisticated versions of it. I need a larger team. I need more time. I need the technology to improve.

Every new generation of AI makes those explanations weaker.

Abundance creates its own tragedy: people can have every means to create and still remain unable to choose what deserves their commitment.

When intelligence is cheap, desire becomes precious.

Knowing what you care about, what you want to express, and which problem you are willing to spend years solving will matter more than knowing how to execute every step yourself.

## Humanity gets to ask a better question

The hierarchy of needs was never a moral ranking. A person fighting for survival is not less evolved than someone writing poetry. They are responding intelligently to the conditions life gave them.

Our responsibility is to change those conditions.

Progress means reducing the amount of human life consumed by involuntary survival. Better energy, medicine, automation, education, and institutions give people back hours that scarcity used to steal.

AI can accelerate that movement. It can also concentrate wealth and power if we build it badly. Better technology does not guarantee abundance. We must deliberately organize technology to produce it.

If we succeed, cheaper products and shorter workweeks will be the least interesting results. Billions of people will get to meet themselves earlier.

Children will not have to wait until retirement to ask what they are here to create. Adults will not have to earn the right to be curious. More people will begin with self-expression and build the rest of their lives around what they discover.

For thousands of years, only a few people had the freedom to reach the top of the pyramid.

The next chapter of human progress is to make the top a place from which anyone can start.
