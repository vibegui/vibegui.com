The race stopped being about which model you use. It is about who builds the factory around it.

The last three years look like fashion. They are not. Each year we learned to hand the machine one more thing, and each thing got a name. Here they are in order, and what each one actually changed.

<FactoryTimeline locale="en" />

## Prompt engineering: we got better at asking

You type a question, you get an answer. If the answer is bad, you rewrite the question.

This was the whole job in 2023. People were hired for it. Anthropic advertised a Prompt Engineer role in January that year at [$250k to $335k](https://web.archive.org/web/20230118233623/https://jobs.lever.co/Anthropic/e3cde481-d446-460f-b576-93cab67bd1ed).

It stopped being the job when the models got good enough to not need the trick. OpenAI's own documentation now tells you to stop: ["Avoid chain-of-thought prompts: Since these models perform reasoning internally, prompting them to 'think step by step' or 'explain your reasoning' is unnecessary."](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/reasoning-best-practices)

## Agentic workflows: it stopped answering and started working

March 2024, Andrew Ng: ["I think AI agentic workflows will drive massive AI progress this year — perhaps even more than the next generation of foundation models."](https://x.com/AndrewYNg/status/1770897666702233815)

The change is small to describe and large in practice. Instead of answering once, the model plans, calls a tool, reads the result, and tries again. It loops.

Ng was right that the loop mattered more than the next model. That is the first time anyone said the machine around the model was the point.

## Vibe coding: we stopped reading the output

February 2025, Karpathy: ["you fully give in to the vibes, embrace exponentials, and forget that the code even exists."](https://x.com/karpathy/status/1886192184808149383) He meant it for weekend projects.

It did not stay there. A month later a quarter of Y Combinator's Winter 2025 batch had [95% of their code written by AI](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/06/a-quarter-of-startups-in-ycs-current-cohort-have-codebases-that-are-almost-entirely-ai-generated/). Collins made it word of the year.

What changed: output volume stopped being the constraint. What broke: nobody was reading it.

## Context engineering: we showed it our business

June 2025, and it came from a merchant rather than a lab. Tobi Lütke: ["the art of providing all the context for the task to be plausibly solvable by the LLM."](https://x.com/tobi/status/1935533422589399127)

This is where most companies still are, and it is why your pilot underwhelmed. The vendor's demo worked because their agent knew their situation. Yours does not know your catalog rules, your merchandising logic, or the one checkout exception for the São Paulo warehouse.

Your documentation debt stopped being untidy and became a line item.

## Harness engineering: we let it act

By late 2025 the missing piece was not knowledge. It was permission.

A harness is what the agent is allowed to touch: the terminal, the repository, your admin API, your analytics, your error tracker. An agent with those can do work. An agent without them is an expensive colleague who may only give advice.

Nobody coined this word. It arrived unclaimed because nobody set out to invent the category. And it is already finished as a differentiator — Anthropic published MCP in November 2024, and thirteen months later it was [handed to the Linux Foundation](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/linux-foundation-announces-the-formation-of-the-agentic-ai-foundation) with AWS, Google, Microsoft and OpenAI on the board. Ten thousand servers. It is plumbing now.

## Context graphs: we let it remember

December 2025. Context stops being a pile of files and becomes a structure — entities, decisions, history, connected.

The difference is practical. A pile of files has to be re-read. A graph can be searched for precedent: what did we try on this category last quarter, and what happened.

Without this, every agent starts from zero every morning.

## The software factory: we gave it a goal

And this is the one that is happening now. Everything above makes the machine better at doing what it was told. A factory changes who tells it.

I should say plainly that I did not invent the term. Bemer proposed a software factory at General Electric in 1968 and Hitachi built one in 1969. It meant industrialising human programmers — standard tools, reuse, variance control. If you are over forty-five you may remember it as a bad memory.

The word is being reused because the shape finally fits. Bemer wanted a factory and only had people to put in it.

<FactoryPlant locale="en" />

The inputs are not tickets. They are signals: a supplier feed lands with broken images, a competitor drops price on your top SKUs, Tuesday's deploy costs you conversion by Thursday. The system watches, decides what is worth building, ships it, tests it on live traffic, keeps the answer, and goes again.

Someone still writes the goal and the constraints. That part did not move.

## The model is not the differentiator anymore

Here is the state of play this month. Anthropic's Opus 5 landed on 24 July at half the price of its own flagship. OpenAI's GPT-5.6 family arrived on 9 July. Grok 4.6 shipped two days ago. Llama is gone — Meta replaced it with a closed line, then released a small open model three days ago.

Now the two numbers that matter.

<FactoryGap locale="en" />

On raw intelligence, the best open model and the best closed model are basically tied. Sixty against sixty-three.

Give them an actual job — multi-step work in a terminal, the kind an agent does all day — and it is 58.7 against 83.8.

Same models. The closed labs ship the machine around the model. The open weights arrive as just a model.

## What the numbers say about money

You have been promised a lot. Here is what is measured.

US productivity has barely moved: [0.07% over the four quarters to March](https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/data-and-indicators/total-factor-productivity-tfp/). The St. Louis Fed read 490,000 earnings calls and found [95% of AI productivity talk is in the future tense](https://www.stlouisfed.org/on-the-economy/2026/jul/ai-productivity-what-firms-say-earnings-calls). The Atlanta Fed asked 750 CFOs what AI did for them, then computed it from their own reported numbers: they [said 1.8% and their figures implied 0.6%](https://www.atlantafed.org/-/media/Project/Atlanta/FRBA/Documents/research/publication/working-paper/2026/03/25/04-artificial-intelligence-productivity-and-the-workforce-evidence-from-corporate-executives.pdf).

Three times more felt than measured. Remember that when a vendor shows you a case study.

Now the part I did not expect. That same study found the firms with real gains did not get them by cutting costs — cutting labour and cutting other costs were the two lowest-ranked motives on the list. The gains came from new products and serving customers better. And where MIT found real savings, they came from [cancelling BPO contracts and cutting agency spend by 30%](https://mlq.ai/media/quarterly_decks/v0.1_State_of_AI_in_Business_2025_Report.pdf), "without material workforce reduction."

So the money is not in firing your team. It is in the invoices you send outside, and in revenue you could not reach before.

On revenue, the signal is already in your data. Adobe measured AI-referred retail visits converting [54% higher](https://business.adobe.com/resources/sdk/.q3-ai-traffic-trends-report/q3-2026-ai-sourced-traffic-insights.pdf) than everything else, growing 138% a year. A year ago those visits were worth less than half a normal one.

Two honest caveats. That traffic is still a fraction of a percent of sessions. And retail is the slowest sector to adopt any of this — [14% of retail firms use AI](https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2026/05/ai-use-businesses.html) against 19.8% across the economy. The demand arrived before the operation did.

## Building one is the new race

You will not win this on model choice. That decision has a shelf life of about six weeks and everyone in this room will make roughly the same one.

You win it on how fast you can stand up a loop that watches your operation, decides what is worth building, ships it, proves it on live traffic, and remembers the answer.

We are building exactly that for digital experience, at [decocms.com](https://decocms.com/?utm_source=vibegui.com&utm_medium=article&utm_campaign=state-of-applied-ai).

The people who spend the next year comparing models will have a spreadsheet. The people who spend it building the factory will have a factory.
