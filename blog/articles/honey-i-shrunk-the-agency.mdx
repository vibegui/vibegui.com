For years we built tools meant to constrain people into shipping high-quality digital experiences — CMS workflows, approval gates, software full of best practices that assumed the human would finish the job if we narrowed the path enough.

People worked around the rails. Best practices became checkboxes. The backlog still grew. Someone deployed a catalog change, conversion dipped, and nobody noticed for a week. We had made the path narrower. We had not made the site better.

Eventually we admitted the premise was wrong. Quality doesn't come from corralling people. It comes from a reliable system that continuously monitors the experience, finds what's broken or leaving money on the table, fixes it, proves it on live traffic, and ships again.

So we stopped building tools and started doing the work: shrink the agency into software that runs that loop on the site overnight.

## Relevance decays faster than humans ship

Brands that sell through a site and an app are in a constant fight to stay relevant. Core Web Vitals slip. Catalog drifts. Campaigns collide. Competitors ship overnight. Black Friday is a load test written by God. The backlog never clears — even with an agency and an in-house squad already on payroll.

No customer writes in to say the mobile PDP got slower. They just don't buy. Someone has to catch that conversion fell after Tuesday's catalog deploy, open a branch, ship the fix, and check GMV in the morning while the rest of the pipeline keeps selling. Cover all of it — software, SEO, CRO, UX, frontend, QA, analytics — and you're carrying seven roles, tens of thousands a month, forever.

<StoryDecay locale="en" />

Most entrepreneurs buy two of those seven and hope. The brands that can afford all seven don't escape either: every new SKU, channel, and landing page is more surface that can rot, and the team maintaining it grows linearly at best. The backlog doubles. The queue still moves at the speed of meetings.

## The agency already knew how

We've spent years living inside digital services agencies — ours and our customers'. The model is not mysterious.

There's an account manager. There's a Kanban. Specialists pick up cards: SEO, CRO, frontend, QA, content. Someone opens a PR. Someone deploys. Someone looks at the numbers and argues about what matters next week.

That loop is how serious brands fight relevance decay. It works when the team is sharp and the board is honest. It just can't outrun decay on human hours.

Sequoia did the math: for every dollar a company spends on software, roughly six go to services. Software founders spent a decade fighting over the smaller pile.

Nothing about the work itself changed. What agencies do is what brands need — it just could never be sold at software margins, because it was made of people, and people don't scale. They sleep.

That's the shift. AI agents make the work sellable like software. So we started selling the work, not the tool.

## The agency fits in a room

That is the shrink. The same roles — PO, SEO, CRO, frontend, QA, content — running as agents on the same Kanban, having the same argument about what matters next week. They pick up cards, open PRs, ship, watch the numbers. Overnight, at software speed.

We got the interface wrong for a long time. Dashboards. One big prompt box. A blob you configure. It worked, and nobody wanted to use it, because it didn't feel like hiring anyone — it felt like operating a machine.

What finally worked looks like Slack. That's [deco Studio](https://www.decocms.com/studio): a room per project, a thread per piece of work, agents you pull in by name. You @ the SEO agent the way you'd @ a colleague. It answers, takes the card, and the whole story of the work stays in the thread — the diagnosis, the argument, the PR, the deploy preview, the number the next morning.

Nobody needed training. Everyone already knows how to talk to an agency in a chat window.

<StoryRooms locale="en" />

Take away prioritization and you don't have an agency, you have a slot machine opening random PRs. What disappears is the week between "let's fix this" and "it's live": sandbox, coding agent, PR, QA, deploy preview. Deciding what deserves a card is still yours.

## Each turn makes the model stronger

Self-evolving here is literal. Every fix ships as an experiment: the agent changes something, live traffic answers, and the answer gets kept. A win and a loss are both worth keeping.

That is what a senior specialist really is — someone who has watched a few hundred of those answers and can guess the next one. In an agency that knowledge lives in three or four heads, and it walks out the door when they do.

Here it stays. Every brand the factory runs adds to the same pile: which PDP layout held up on mobile, which copy moved a category, which obvious speed fix did nothing at all. The thousandth store starts with what the first nine hundred and ninety-nine already learned.

So the agency you hire next year is better than the one you hire today, and nobody pays for that lesson twice.
