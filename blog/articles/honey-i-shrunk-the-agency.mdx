For years we built tools meant to constrain people into shipping high-quality digital experiences — CMS workflows, approval gates, software full of best practices that assumed the human would finish the job if we narrowed the path enough.

People worked around the rails. Best practices became checkboxes. The backlog still grew. Someone deployed a catalog change, conversion dipped, and nobody noticed for a week. We had made the path narrower. We had not made the store better.

Eventually we admitted the premise was wrong. Quality doesn't come from corralling people. It comes from a reliable system that continuously monitors the experience, finds what's broken or leaving money on the table, fixes it, proves it on live traffic, and ships again.

So we stopped building rails and started doing the work: shrink the agency into software that runs that loop on the store overnight.

## Relevance decays faster than you can hire

Brands moving billions through site and app are in a constant fight to stay relevant. Core Web Vitals slip. Catalog drifts. Campaigns collide. Competitors ship overnight. Black Friday is a load test written by God. The backlog never clears — even with an agency and an in-house squad already on payroll.

"Can AI write a banner" was never their problem. Their problem is whether the operation that owns conversion can move faster than decay — and whether that operation only exists for brands that can staff a fifteen-person digital services bench forever.

Who notices PDP conversion fell on mobile after a catalog deploy, opens a branch, ships a fix, and checks GMV in the morning — while the rest of the pipeline keeps selling?

That is the seat I care about. The factory that runs the experience that prints the money.

<StoryDecay locale="en" />

A full agency package is expensive. Software, SEO, CRO, UX, frontend, QA, analytics. Tens of thousands a month for the real bench. Mid-market founders often can't buy that cast at all.

As the brand grows, managing it gets *worse*, not better. More SKUs. More channels. More pages that can rot. More people in Slack arguing about priority. The backlog doubles while the queue moves at the speed of meetings. Different budgets, same physics: more work than people, and degradation faster than shipping.

## The agency already knew how

We've spent years living inside digital services agencies — ours and everyone else's. The model is not mysterious.

There's an account manager. There's a Kanban. Specialists pick up cards: SEO, CRO, frontend, QA, content. Someone opens a PR. Someone deploys. Someone looks at the numbers and argues about what matters next week.

That loop is how serious brands fight relevance. It works when the team is sharp and the board is honest. It also doesn't scale with human hours alone.

I read that and thought: we already sell the work. We've been the agency for these pipelines. What we need is the same roles, the same loop, at a software price point — so a brand fighting decay doesn't wait on another squad every time the backlog doubles.

## The agency fits in a room

That is the shrink. Same cast — PO, SEO, CRO, frontend, QA, content — running as a team of agents. Same Kanban. Same fight over what matters next week. They pick up cards, open PRs, ship, watch the numbers. Overnight. At a software price point.

You don't manage a blob. You talk to an agency. Pull a specialist into a thread the way you'd Slack QA. Prioritize on a board. One conversation surface per codebase.

<StoryRooms locale="en" />

Without prioritization you don't have an agency. You have a slot machine that opens random PRs. Judgment stays on what gets a card. The grind — sandbox, coding agent, PR, QA, deploy preview — compresses.

## Each turn makes the model stronger

Self-evolving here is literal. That loop runs day and night: monitor what's costing conversion, fix it in production, keep the evidence across brands, learn and evolve every turn.

Agencies promise continuous operation against decay. Agents can actually deliver it — 24/7.

## What we're shipping

Paste a URL, get a public score. Connect data, get a benchmark against operations we've already run. The board turns findings into cards. Autofix runs the rest. Diagnostics free. Limited free autofix. Then you pay for the factory to keep watching.

The work still lands on GitHub — pull requests, deploy previews, QA gates. A human still owns the gold cards. Skip that and you just ship bugs faster.

We've lived inside the slow, expensive version of this for years. Agents take the grind. Judgment stays on what gets a card.
