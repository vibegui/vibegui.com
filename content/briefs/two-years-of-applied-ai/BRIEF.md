# Brief: AI Learned to Work. Now We Need to Build the Factory.

**Slug**: two-years-of-applied-ai
**Type**: long MDX story, written to work as an 8–10 minute talk
**Created**: 2026-08-13
**Revised**: 2026-08-16
**Occasion**: deco House, Monday 2026-08-17, for ecommerce and technology executives

## The one point

> AI evolved from answering to executing. A software factory completes the transition by letting a system operate continuously against a human goal.

The last two years did not produce a sequence of disconnected AI fashions. Tools, agents, MCP, context, harnesses, and memory transferred one responsibility at a time from the person in front of the screen to the system behind it.

## Audience and job

The audience knows AI terminology but needs an operating model. The piece should let an executive identify one loop worth building on Monday, then understand why deco is building a software factory for digital experience.

It must work aloud without slides and remain useful as a standalone article afterward. Keep the language concrete enough that a listener can retell the factory in terms of its goal, signals, internal loops, outputs, feedback, and accountable human.

## Structure

1. **The lived gap** — deco made AI understand businesses, but useful answers still waited in a chat window.
2. **Answer** — prompting improved one step while the human carried the rest of the work.
3. **Execute** — tools, loops, MCP, context, and harnesses made multistep action possible.
4. **Execution is not operation** — broad AI adoption hides shallow workflow change; introduce four criteria for a viable loop.
5. **The software factory** — explain goal and constraints, signals as inputs, internal loops, actions as outputs, feedback and memory, and human accountability.
6. **One running ecommerce example** — a PDP regression travels through the entire factory from conversion signal to measured recovery.
7. **The bridge** — one sentence connecting the operating model to the digital-experience factory at decocms.com.

## Editorial rules

- Target 1,200–1,350 spoken words, including a natural pause for each visual.
- Prefer the PDP regression example over lists of unrelated use cases.
- Every statistic must change the listener's decision, not merely demonstrate research.
- Do not describe memory as synonymous with a context graph.
- Do not claim the model is irrelevant; the argument is that model capability alone does not operate a business.
