I am building a world where everyone keeps their declared future in git — versioned, forkable, reviewable — and runs a machine that turns it into content and code.

My own life is the first instance. Every project I care about has a place in the whole: a declared outcome, an honest current state, a memory, and a next move I can make today. The people who matter stay present, with enough real context to know when to reconnect, offer help, or ask for it. The books, people, and projects that shaped me are a living library instead of a pile of content, and my files are findable instead of scattered across three drives. The distinctions I wish I'd had at 25 are available through a conversation that lands — and what someone gets from one becomes something they can share.

Not a faster factory. A factory that knows what it is for.

Two scores, no others: alignment and integrity. Alignment: does what I am doing serve the future I declared. Integrity: is it whole and complete — my word, my systems, my objects. Everything else is machinery, and machinery follows.

From Rio, in the open — because a method nobody can copy is a hobby, and a method its author will not live inside is a lie.
