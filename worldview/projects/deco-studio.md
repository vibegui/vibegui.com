**Spirit:** the tool I wish I had when I was starting out.

## Declared outcome

Make the open-source AI platform legible, useful, and adopted.

Studio is a factory: the place a person states a goal and a team of agents turns
it into inspectable work. Runtime is an attempt at the same function. They are
parallel bets, and Studio is the one with users, maintainers, and a team
depending on it — which is both its advantage and the reason it cannot cheaply
gamble on its own shape.

Three declared outcomes depend on it, and they are different claims:

**`software-factory`** — Studio is one of the two candidates. The outcome does not
care which wins; it requires a queue derived from the gap rather than typed into a
box, and Studio is the incumbent attempt at that.

**`multiplication`** — *anyone can keep their declared future in git.* The
outcome's own criterion is that a person begins "without becoming an
infrastructure expert." Studio is that clause, and it is the only project that
plausibly satisfies it: it is the one with adoption. Runtime is an internal
exploration and serves nothing here yet.

**`agency`** — *my projects move as one portfolio.* Somewhere has to be the place
I sit every day. Studio is where the MCP App renders and where a worldview and a
factory get connected today.

## Success criteria

1. Clear positioning plus active users and maintainers.
2. A person connects a Worldview and begins without reading source.
3. I use it daily, and the daily brief actually gets reviewed — the criterion that
   has failed longest.
4. The backlog is triaged rather than accumulating.

## Where it actually is

Active. **397 open work items.** That number is the finding, and it is
self-referential: a backlog that size makes "legible" false at the level of
Studio's own repository, while its declared outcome is to make things legible. One
open capture since 2026-07-18 asks for exactly this — triage, categorise, identify
what blocks adoption.

Not checked out on this machine. `~/Projects/deco-sites/decocms` is the marketing
site (889 commits, last touched 2026-06-17), a different repository from
`decocms/studio` — worth not confusing when reading evidence.

## Competing with Runtime, on purpose

Runtime exists so that ideas which would be reckless in Studio — the rooms
format, org design as a pull request — can be tried where no team is depending on
the answer. That is a deliberate hedge, so two projects serving
`software-factory` is not a duplication error.

The open question is Studio's side of the same problem Runtime has: **what
evidence ends the race, and when do I look?** Studio's advantage is real users;
Runtime's is freedom to be wrong. Neither converges on its own, and the default
outcome of an undecided race is both continuing indefinitely.

Whatever Runtime proves has to come back here, or Runtime replaces it. Learning
that stays in the exploration repo was spent for nothing.

## Not the same thing as `decocms`

`deco-studio` is a product I maintain with a declared outcome I am accountable
for, mapped and scored. `decocms` is the company commitment: a row that exists so
the portfolio does not pretend my primary professional attention is absent,
deliberately unscored because company operations do not enter this system.
Tracking Studio under it would hide a project three of my declared outcomes
depend on inside a row excluded from alignment.
